SB 79 in Palo Alto. What's happening, newest first.

California's new law lets developers build taller apartment buildings near major train stations — including all three Caltrain stations in Palo Alto. This page is the running record of how that is playing out: council votes, project filings, state reviews, lawsuits, and our own corrections. Every item links to its source and says how well it is verified.

  1. Sep 10, 2026 Neighbors · San Carlos City record

    San Carlos says the state signed off on its SB 79 exclusion in June

    A staff work plan filed for the September 14 San Carlos Council meeting marks every step of the city's SB 79 ordinance complete, ending with: “Approved by HCD on June 19, 2026.” That is the first statement from any source that the state housing department acted on San Carlos's site exclusion (Ordinance 1634, adopted in April). The same document notes that the “unanticipated work from SB 79 exemption” pushed another downtown project back five months.

    We have not seen the HCD letter itself, and “approved” is the city's word. Under the law, HCD's review is a check for substantial compliance, not a permit.

    Source: San Carlos Council agenda, Sep 14, 2026 (Strategic Work Plan update, Attachment 2). Background: San Carlos on the neighbors page.

  2. Sep 8, 2026 State · HCD State record

    HCD spells out which rules a project follows: the city's, unless the state says otherwise

    The state housing department (HCD) added a “TOD Project Compliance” section to its SB 79 page, dated August 19. It says a housing project follows the local implementing ordinance unless HCD determines that ordinance is out of compliance — in which case projects fall back to SB 79's own standards. For Palo Alto, that means the half-size rule governs new applications unless and until HCD finds fault with it. HCD has said nothing about Palo Alto's ordinances so far.

    Source: HCD, Transit-Oriented Development (SB 79), “TOD Project Compliance.” We compared the page against archived copies to confirm the section is new.

  3. Sep 8, 2026 Palo Alto · Council City record

    Found in the archive: Council first took up SB 79 in October 2025

    Our meeting log began in May 2026. It turns out Council held its first SB 79 discussion on October 22, 2025, five weeks after the governor signed the bill, as Item 4 of a special meeting on the Downtown Housing Plan. Both sets of minutes record no action. Staff's report floated a transit-oriented alternative plan and, “potentially,” a Council ad hoc committee. The SB 79 ad hoc (Burt and Lauing) was at work by early 2026.

    Sources: agenda · action minutes · video. Now the first entry in the Council watch meeting log.

  4. Sep 7, 2026 Neighbors · Los Altos Correction

    Los Altos does have an SB 79 analysis on record. It answers a narrower question than ours.

    We said Los Altos had never publicly analyzed SB 79. Wrong: a city-commissioned ballot-measure impact report, received by Council on June 23, states that “there are no major transit stops that are eligible for SB 79 … in Los Altos.” That is about stops located inside the city. Our open question is different: whether the half-mile band around San Antonio Caltrain, a station in Mountain View, reaches into north Los Altos. The report does not settle that. Council received the report unanimously; receiving is not adopting.

    Sources: June 23 Council packet (§9212 report, p. 232) and the approved minutes. See Los Altos on the neighbors page.

  5. Sep 6, 2026 Elsewhere · Los Angeles City record

    Los Angeles adopted its SB 79 ordinance 14–0

    The LA City Clerk's council file shows the phased-implementation ordinance passed on June 23, 2026 by a 14–0–1 vote (one member absent), after an earlier 13–0–2 vote on June 3. LA is the largest city to use SB 79's deferral tools, and its ordinance is the one that enumerates all six statutory exclusion categories — a useful reference for what Palo Alto's own rules could and could not do.

    Source: Council File 25-1083-S4, vote information block.

  6. Sep 6, 2026 Palo Alto · Stakeholders City record

    The Housing Action Coalition's letter, retrieved from the June 15 packet

    The pro-housing group's June 10 letter to Council sits at pages 45–49 of the June 15 public-comment attachment. It opposes the half-size rule and makes a claim our meeting log does not have: that on June 1 Council voted down an amendment from Councilmember Lythcott-Haims. Our record of June 1 shows a 5–0 vote with two friendly amendments and no failed motion. We are treating the HAC account as unverified until we check the summary minutes and video.

    Sources: June 15 Item 23 public-comment attachment (city record) · HAC's SB 79 page.

  7. Sep 6, 2026 Palo Alto · Stakeholders City record

    Two housing-law groups told Council its SB 79 package was legally vulnerable, before the vote

    A joint letter from Californians for Homeownership and the California Housing Defense Fund, filed into the June 15 record, argued that the urgency route was not available, that the half-size rule's building envelope could preclude the density the law requires, and that the city's environmental-review exemption did not apply. It attached a 2023 Attorney General letter on urgency ordinances. The same two groups have since sued San Francisco, San Diego and Montebello over their SB 79 ordinances. They have not sued Palo Alto.

    Source: June 15 Item 23 public comment (city record). Now under Stakeholder positions.

  8. Aug 30, 2026 Palo Alto · Council Clarification

    Which “Palo Alto Forward letter” did Council mean on June 1?

    Council's June 1 friendly amendment directed staff to weigh “the Palo Alto Forward letter” when drafting permanent rules. We had only one PAF letter on file, from April 30, addressed to a different agenda item. A second PAF letter, dated May 29 and headed “RE: Agenda Item 17, Implementation of SB 79,” matches the item Council was voting on. It redlines the city's proposal with eight carve-outs where full SB 79 would apply. We now link the May 29 letter as the one Council meant. That is our inference, not confirmed from the video.

    Source: PAF letter, May 29, 2026 · June 1 meeting video.

  9. Aug 28, 2026 Palo Alto · Council City record

    First look at the half-size rule on an actual parcel, and a site inventory tagged “SB 79”

    The September 9 Council packet is the first city document to show what the new TOD Combining District does to one lot. For 470 Olive Avenue (a separate rezoning, not an SB 79 project), the overlay sets 55 ft height, 70% lot coverage, a minimum of 30 homes per acre, and reduced setbacks. The staff report says the overlay raises allowable floor area to 1.75; its own attachment and the staff presentation say 1.5. That contradiction is unresolved. Separately, an information report shows the city amended its Housing Element site inventory on August 27, with 14 sites labeled “SB 79: Yes” or “No.”

    Source: Sept 9 Council agenda, Item 12 and Information Report A.

  10. Aug 24, 2026 Courts Press report

    Three cities now sued over SB 79 ordinances: San Francisco, San Diego, Montebello

    Californians for Homeownership and the California Housing Defense Fund announced suits against San Diego (case 26CU043967C) and Montebello (26STCP02862), alongside the San Francisco case (CPF-26-520062). The San Diego theory is the one a city Palo Alto's size could face: alleged misuse of the “no walking path within a mile” exemption, which has no minimum-stop-count prerequisite. Nothing in Palo Alto's record shows the city used that exemption. No petition has been read yet; the case numbers come from the plaintiffs' release.

    Sources: CalHDF release · PR Newswire · Smart Cities Dive. See the FAQ on lawsuits.

  11. Aug 24, 2026 Palo Alto · Correction Correction

    Palo Alto's temporary rules expire July 1, 2027, not 2032

    Both June 15 ordinances carry the same sentence: they “shall expire on July 1, 2027 unless extended or superseded.” This site had been publishing only the other clock in the same documents, the statutory ceiling of roughly 2032 tied to the next Housing Element. Both are real, but the operative instrument runs out in ten months unless Council acts. Two later city documents say the same: a planning-commission memo and the city's own applicant handout, headed “effective through July 1, 2027.” Corrected on four pages.

    Sources: June 15 Item 23, Attachment A §6 and Attachment B §7 (city record); PTC Aug 26 Item 3 memo; city TOD Combining District handout.

  12. Aug 16, 2026 Palo Alto · Planning commission City record

    The law is shaping projects that aren't using it: 332 Forest advances 4–2

    A builder brought two rival plans for the same downtown site to the planning commission on August 12: a larger discretionary project (82 homes, 16 demolitions) and a smaller SB 79 fallback filed during the July window. The applicant called the SB 79 filing “an insurance policy.” Commissioners said on the record that the fallback limited what they could ask for. They voted 4–2 to send the larger plan to the Architectural Review Board, Chang and Ji dissenting. The draft minutes confirm the vote and unit count reported by Palo Alto Online.

    Sources: PTC draft summary minutes · Palo Alto Online · video.

  13. Aug 15, 2026 Neighbors · Mountain View City record

    Mountain View moves to exempt 24 historic properties from SB 79

    Beyond its long-planned local alternative plan, Mountain View is pursuing the statute's historic-resource exclusion: an ordinance plus a zoning-map amendment covering 24 properties, which went to the Environmental Planning Commission on August 19. It is a narrower tool than Palo Alto's half-size rule and does not touch the rest of the city's station areas. The commission's outcome is not yet in the public record; a Council hearing is tentatively set for September 22.

    Sources: EPC agenda · staff report. See Mountain View on the neighbors page.

  14. Aug 14, 2026 Courts Press report

    First SB 79 lawsuit runs the other way: housing groups sue San Francisco

    Our FAQ predicted a city would sue the state. The first case is the inverse: YIMBY Law, the California Housing Defense Fund and Californians for Homeownership sued San Francisco (and named HCD) on August 13, arguing the city over-used SB 79's industrial-area exemption and unlawfully barred projects from stacking the state density bonus. HCD's own July 1 review letter had already flagged the density-bonus clause as impermissible. The industrial exemption requires at least 15 transit stops, so that theory cannot reach Palo Alto; the density-bonus question can.

    Sources: SF Standard · Davis Vanguard · HCD review letter to San Francisco, July 1, 2026.

  15. Aug 12, 2026 Palo Alto · City City record

    The city published its own account of the temporary rules, and calls the window filings “pre-applications”

    An August 4 City Manager statement confirms the three floor-area limits this site had reported (2.25, 1.75 and 1.5 times the lot, by distance from the station), spells out setback and step-down standards for the first time, and lists the nine July filings by address with a total of 341 homes. The city calls them preliminary applications: each must be followed by a formal application within 180 days before any hearing. The setback and step-down standards apply only where the underlying zoning is stricter, so downtown's zero-foot setbacks survive.

    Source: Status of the City's Temporary SB 79 Implementation Regulations, City of Palo Alto.

  16. Aug 11, 2026 State · Statute Correction

    The off-ramp statute has six categories, not two

    Our neighbors page told readers there were “two ways in” to SB 79's temporary exclusions. The codified text of Government Code §65912.161(b)(1) lists six, lettered (A) through (F): sites that already allow half of SB 79's density, three kinds of low-resource or already-dense zones, sites under a local alternative plan, high fire-hazard areas, sea-level-rise areas, and historic resources. Palo Alto, Menlo Park and San Carlos all used (A); Mountain View and Sunnyvale are using (F). The page now says so.

    Source: Gov. Code §65912.161, read verbatim.

  17. Aug 7, 2026 Neighbors · Atherton Press report

    Atherton's first SB 79 filing: about 30 townhomes at 110 Glenwood

    The Almanac reported that AlphaX RE Capital filed an application under SB 79 on July 9 for 30 townhomes, six of them below market rate, at 110 Glenwood Avenue near the Atherton Caltrain station. The Town's own planning-commission minutes from June 24 had already recorded staff announcing “a potential SB 79 application for 110 Glenwood Avenue … approximately 30 stacked townhome units.” The filing date and the affordable split come from the press, not the Town.

    Sources: The Almanac · Atherton Planning Commission draft minutes, June 24 (in the July 22 packet).

  18. Aug 6, 2026 Neighbors · Atherton City record

    Atherton's ordinance is No. 678, adopted 5–0, and HCD found it in compliance

    The Town's approved March 18 minutes give what its web pages did not: Ordinance 678 passed on the consent calendar, 5–0, on a Lewis/Lane motion. Atherton's ordinance adds objective processing standards and leaves SB 79's minimums untouched. HCD's June 10 review letter, posted on the Town's own site, found it in substantial compliance.

    Sources: approved minutes, Mar 18, 2026 · HCD letter, Jun 10, 2026.

  19. Aug 5, 2026 Palo Alto · Stakeholders Allegation

    SB 79's sponsor says Palo Alto is “in clear violation of state housing law”

    California YIMBY's one-month review of the law names Palo Alto, with Beverly Hills, for keeping SB 79's limit on density-bonus height in a local plan. The argument assumes Palo Alto adopted a local alternative plan. The city's record shows it did something else: a site exclusion under §65912.161(b), with the alternative plan still a future step. The city's September 9 staff presentation also says the TOD district standards “may be further modified by State Density Bonus Law.” HCD has written nothing about Palo Alto, and no one has sued. This is an advocacy claim, recorded as one.

    Source: California YIMBY, “SB 79, One Month In”.

  20. Jul 30, 2026 Palo Alto · Projects Press report

    First project after the window: 98 homes on Park Blvd, and a fight over which rules apply

    A seven-story, 98-unit building would replace three office buildings at 2151–2211 Park Boulevard, a quarter mile from the California Avenue station. The builder dated its application July 16, the day the half-size rule took effect, and asks to be judged by full SB 79. A city spokesperson told Palo Alto Online the plans reached the permit portal on July 28 and the local rule therefore applies. The gap is concrete: the plan proposes 2.7 times the lot in floor area where the city's cap is 1.75. Not yet checked against city permit records.

    Source: Palo Alto Online.

  21. Jul 29, 2026 Neighbors · San Carlos City record

    San Carlos wasn't silent: it switched SB 79 off around its station in April

    We had San Carlos down as taking no action. In fact it adopted Ordinance 1634 on April 13, effective May 13, excluding four dense downtown zoning districts within a quarter mile of the Caltrain station from SB 79 entirely, until a year after the next Housing Element. This is a different tool from Palo Alto's: Palo Alto halved SB 79's building size on its station parcels; San Carlos removed the parcels that already allow half of SB 79's density. The approved minutes show the vote was 3–0 on consent, two members absent.

    Sources: SCMC Chapter 18.25A · Apr 13 agenda · approved minutes.

  22. Jul 28, 2026 Neighbors · Redwood City Press report

    Redwood City chose the state standards outright and ordered up an alternative plan

    Four press accounts agree that on July 13 the Redwood City Council unanimously declined any off-ramp, let SB 79's standards stand as written, and directed staff to prepare a transit-oriented alternative plan alongside its downtown plan, targeted for spring 2027. Our page had called the city silent. The city's own agenda portal is not machine-readable, so this rests on reporting until someone opens the July 13 record.

    Sources: Local News Matters · RWC Pulse · Patch.

  23. Jul 26, 2026 Neighbors · Los Altos Correction

    Los Altos may be too small for SB 79's outer band to reach it

    We described the half-mile band from San Antonio Caltrain as “spilling into” north Los Altos. The statute's quarter-to-half-mile standards apply only “within a city with a population of at least 35,000.” Los Altos has 31,921 residents by the state's January 2026 estimate. No part of Los Altos is within a quarter mile of the station, so the outer band may not reach it at all. Whether such a city is simply outside SB 79 or falls under a lower default standard is an open question we have not resolved.

    Sources: Gov. Code §65912.157(a)(4) and (a)(6), read verbatim; Department of Finance E-1 estimates.

  24. Jul 25, 2026 State · MTC Correction

    The regional station map is still labeled a preliminary draft

    Seven pages on this site said the Bay Area's SB 79 station map became official on July 1, 2026. As of August it is still labeled a “preliminary draft” by the regional planning agencies, and no adoption record has surfaced. Under the statute the map carries a rebuttable presumption of validity regardless of its label. Our own count of trains per station, which puts all three Palo Alto stations in the top tier, does not depend on the map.

    Sources: MTC SB 79 Regional Map · ABAG SB 79 page. Method: how we counted the trains.

  25. Jul 23, 2026 Palo Alto · Projects City record

    Nine projects, 341 homes, filed in the two-week window

    The count of projects filed between July 1 and July 15, when full-strength SB 79 applied near the stations, ended at nine, not the seven we had reported. The last two: 69 apartments at 332 and 350 Forest Avenue downtown, and 19 homes at 2497 High Street near the California Avenue station. First reported by Palo Alto Online; the city's own August 4 statement later listed all nine by address with the same 341-home total.

    Sources: Palo Alto Online · city status statement.

  26. Jul 20, 2026 Neighbors · Menlo Park City record

    Menlo Park's approved minutes: three speakers, all against an exemption

    When Menlo Park's Council declined an SB 79 exemption on May 12, we counted two public speakers from the video. The approved minutes list three: June Shin, Adina Levin and Katherine Dumont, each supporting the planning commission's no-action recommendation. The direction of the record is unchanged: no one who spoke asked the city to opt out.

    Source: approved minutes, May 12, 2026.
